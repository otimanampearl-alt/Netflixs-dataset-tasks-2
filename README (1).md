# Task 2: Netflix Content Distribution Dashboard

## Project Overview

This project uses Microsoft Excel to visualise the distribution of Movies and TV Shows in a Netflix catalogue dataset. The goal is to turn the content-type summary into clear charts and a dashboard that makes the proportions easier to understand.

**Main business question:** How is the Netflix catalogue in this dataset distributed between Movies and TV Shows?

## Dataset

The workbook contains a Netflix dataset with 8,790 title records in the main dataset sheet. The content-type summary in the Pivot Table worksheet reports the following:

| Content Type | Number of Titles | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Movies | 6,126 | 69.7% |
| TV Shows | 2,664 | 30.3% |
| **Total** | **8,790** | **100%** |

## Analysis and Visualisation

The content-type summary was used as the basis for the visual analysis. The workbook includes pie and column/bar chart visualisations intended to compare the number and proportion of Movies and TV Shows, alongside a dashboard worksheet.

The pie chart communicates each content type's share of the total. The column chart supports a direct comparison of the title counts.

## Key Findings

- Movies account for **6,126 titles**, approximately **69.7%** of the dataset.
- TV Shows account for **2,664 titles**, approximately **30.3%**.
- Movies make up the larger share of the catalogue represented in this dataset—about 2.3 times the number of TV Shows.

## Business Insights and Recommendations

The dataset contains a higher number of Movies than TV Shows. This finding could support content portfolio discussions, such as:

- Reviewing whether the current balance between Movies and TV Shows aligns with audience and business objectives.
- Exploring whether there is a strategic opportunity to expand or diversify the TV Show catalogue.
- Combining catalogue data with viewing hours, engagement, production costs and revenue before making investment decisions.

**Important limitation:** Catalogue counts show how many titles are represented in the dataset. They do not establish audience preference, popularity, profitability or viewing demand.

## The Three Data Analyst Questions

### 1. What did I analyse, and what question was I trying to answer?

I analysed the distribution of Netflix titles by content type. The question was: **What proportion of the titles are Movies, and what proportion are TV Shows?**

### 2. What specific finding did the analysis reveal?

Out of 8,790 titles, 6,126 (69.7%) are Movies and 2,664 (30.3%) are TV Shows. Movies therefore represent the larger share of the dataset.

### 3. What action could the findings support?

The findings could support a review of the content portfolio mix and help identify areas for further investigation. Before changing acquisition or production strategy, the counts should be considered alongside audience engagement, viewing performance and financial data.
